# Writing rules for chart pages

These rules apply to every word on a chart page. Read them with `writing-voice.md` (what the text should sound like) and `humanizer.md` (the AI tells to remove). Where they disagree, `writing-voice.md` wins.

## Reader and register

- **Reader:** casual to semi-serious astrology hobbyists. A middle schooler must be able to follow it easily.
- **Talk to the reader as "you".** The page is about one person, and it talks to them.
- **Use everyday words in joined-up sentences.** Link related facts with connectors (and, so, because, while, which) and colons rather than writing runs of short statements. Plain words keep it simple; short sentences don't.
- **Explain any astrology word in passing,** e.g. "Astrologers call Aries an 'away game' for Venus (the word is detriment)."

## What astrology to use

- **Mainstream modern Western astrology only.** Leave out esoteric astrology entirely: Bailey rays, esoteric or hierarchical rulers, mantrams, "soul ray". astro.com output often includes an esoteric section; skip it.
- **Generational planets** (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto and the slow aspects between them): say plainly that everyone born around then shares them, then say why it matters for this person (house, links to personal planets).
- **Retrograde is common and not bad.** Frame it as inward and done in your own way, never as a flaw.
- **Use the mean node and mean Lilith** (astro.com's defaults) and say which one once.

## Facts

- **Invent nothing.** Every fact comes from the astro.com output or from mainstream beginner-friendly sources (Cafe Astrology, AstroTwins, Chani, Astrology King, Wikipedia). Look facts up there and write the copy in your own words; never copy their sentences.
- **Weak sources mean shorter copy.** Where the sources say little (Chiron and Lilith aspects, rare pairings), keep it short and general.
- **Weight notes** from astro.com ("plays only a minor part", "a major part of their psyche", "difficult to express") stay only when the power table and the chart's shape back them up. A singleton (the only planet in an element) or a lone body on one side of the chart is a focus point, so a "minor part" note on it is dropped. Note that Astrolog gives Chiron, Lilith, the node and the angles zero aspect points, so their low power rank says nothing about how connected they are.
- **Name sources exactly,** e.g. "the strength scores on your astro.com report", never a vague "the data".

## Intensity

- **Keep the drama the chart earns.** Plain words, never a flattened paraphrase: the reader should feel awe and recognition. A careful plain-language summary that loses the intensity is a failed rewrite.
- **Read the chart and surface your own findings** for stories, features, cheat codes and questions: patterns across houses, rulers that all lead back to one place, the chart's shape, the Moon phase, the time of day. Don't paraphrase the astro.com sentences.
- **Psychological depth is welcome** (wound, gift, shadow, destiny). Only esoteric material is out.
- **Banned AI-texture words** (figurative uses): carry/carrying, loud/loudest, container, "asks for". See `humanizer.md` §12 for the full list.

## Card structure

Detail cards in the side panel have three parts:

- **What it is:** one general line, the same for every chart (e.g. what Saturn is, what the 7th house covers, what a trine is). Reuse the reference page's lines unless they need fixing.
- **In your chart:** two to five sentences on this person's sign, house and stand-out facts (rulers, aspects, dignity, being alone in an element).
- **Tag:** a short plain label replacing jargon, e.g. "At home in Scorpio · your strongest planet". Use " · " between parts.

## Process

1. Draft with `writing-voice.md`, then run the `humanizer.md` pass on every draft: no dashes, no "not X but Y", no forced groups of three, no one-line closers, no stock AI words.
2. Interpretations go in the conditional (would, may, might); facts (positions, dates, counts) stay plain.
3. Before writing a whole page, show the person three or four sample cards and one story, and get their OK on the voice.
