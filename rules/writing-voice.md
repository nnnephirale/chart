# Writing voice

Distilled from Marilyn's own line edits (astrology chart copy, Oct 2026). Use it on top of the `humanizer` skill: humanizer removes AI tells, this skill decides what the text should sound like instead. Where the two disagree, this skill wins.

## The voice in one paragraph

Essayistic and literary, but readable by anyone: long, joined-up sentences in plain words, with a slightly elevated register and precise verbs. It interprets with confidence but marks interpretation as interpretation. It keeps the stakes high and the feeling real, uses fresh concrete images instead of stock metaphors, and never waters a big idea down to make it simpler. A good reference for the texture is Nell Stevens' *Briefly, a Delicious Life*: wry, intimate, concrete, unafraid of feeling.

## Grammar moves (the systemic part)

These are the structural changes behind her edits. Apply them as defaults, not one-offs. Moves 2, 3 and 4 are the ones she rates strongest; treat them as the core of the voice.

1. **Conditional mood for interpretation.** A reading, prediction or claim about the reader's inner life goes in the conditional or with a modal (*would*, *may*, *might*), which marks it as a reading rather than a reported fact. Facts (positions, dates, numbers, what something *is*) stay in the plain indicative.
   - "finding words for it **is** one of the long projects" → "to find words for it **would be** one of the longer projects"
   - One modal per claim. Stacked hedges ("might possibly perhaps") are still a tell.
2. **Infinitive subjects over gerund subjects.** When an action is the subject of a sentence, prefer the infinitive. It's more formal and literary, and it points forward ("To err is human" register).
   - "Finding words for it would be…" → "To find words for it would be…"
   - "Letting go is the hard part" → "To let go would be the harder part"
3. **The absolute comparative.** Use a comparative with no stated comparison (*the longer projects*, *the deeper parts*, *the older patterns*) instead of a plain or superlative adjective. It implies a scale without making a sweeping claim, the same way "higher education" or "the older generation" does.
   - "one of the long projects" → "one of the longer projects"; "the hardest part" → "one of the harder parts"
4. **Coordination over full stops.** Join related clauses with connectors (*and, so, because, since, while, which, though*) or a colon or semicolon. Avoid runs of short statement-like sentences; plain words do the simplifying, not short sentences.
   - "Empty houses are normal. Your chart simply has a few very busy ones." → "Empty houses are normal, and your chart simply has a few very busy ones."
5. **Precise verbs over personified phrasal ones.** *Requires*, not *asks for*: "that work asks for more solitude" is a strong AI tell. Don't swing to ornate words either; she doesn't love *inhabited* or *metabolized*.
6. **Asides in parentheses, not dashes.** Keep the aside; change the punctuation. "metabolized — not resolved, but inhabited consciously — the gifts" → "metabolized (not resolved, but inhabited consciously), the gifts".
7. **Inner questions in italics.** A question the reader would ask themselves is set in italics: *what am I worth?*

## Shape

- **Framing lines are good.** An invitation to see the whole differently ("Think of 2nd house matters as the center of your chart") lands well, as long as it is fact-checked and doesn't overstate.

## Intensity

- Keep the drama the content earns: stakes, awe, recognition. A careful plain-language paraphrase that loses intensity is a failed rewrite ("a big dilution").
- Insight over restatement: when asked for stories, features or questions, read the source material and surface your own findings rather than paraphrasing existing copy.
- Psychological depth (wound, gift, shadow, destiny) is fine. Accuracy still rules: every claim must be checkable.

## Imagery and words

- Fresh, concrete, slightly wry images, often personified: "a feeling arrives with Mars's heat still on it, Saturn has a hand over its mouth before it's finished speaking".
- **Banned AI-texture words** (figurative uses): carry/carrying, loud/loudest, container(s), "asks for" (personified: "the work asks for"), plus everything in the humanizer's §12 list.
- Name sources exactly ("the strength scores on your astro.com report"), never a vague "X's count".

## Check before handing over

1. Run the humanizer pass.
2. Read it aloud: are interpretations in the conditional and facts in the indicative?
3. Any run of three short sentences that could be joined?
4. Has anything been flattened that the original said with more force?
